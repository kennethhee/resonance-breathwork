How to Identify Your Exact Resonance Rate

1. With a Polar H10 / Garmin / Apple Watch: Pick the rate that produces the largest heart rate oscillation (e.g., HR smoothly rising to 78 bpm on inhale and dropping to 62 bpm on exhale) with a 0° phase relationship.
2. Without a sensor: Pick the slowest rate where you feel zero air hunger, effortless diaphragmatic expansion, and a warm peripheral vasodilation sensation in your hands.

Peer-Reviewed Breathwork Research

1. Cyclic Sighing vs. Box Breathing & Mindfulness (Stanford RCT, 2023)

Balban, Neri, Kogon, Weed, Nouriani, Jo, Holl, Zeitzer, Spiegel & Huberman (2023). A 28-day randomized controlled trial (N=108) comparing 5 minutes/day of cyclic sighing, box breathing, cyclic hyperventilation, and mindfulness meditation. Controlled breathwork—specifically cyclic sighing (double inhale + long exhale)—produced significantly greater daily increases in positive affect and a sustained reduction in resting respiratory rate compared to mindfulness meditation.

Cell Reports Medicine (2023) · PMID: 36630953 · PMC9873947

2. Individual Resonance Frequency vs. Fixed Pacing (Steffen et al., 2017; Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014)

While 5.5 breaths/min (0.092 Hz) is the population median, human closed-loop baroreflex delay varies between 4.5 and 6.5 breaths/min. Steffen et al. (2017) demonstrated that breathing at your exact individual resonance frequency (RF) yields significantly higher LF/HF HRV amplitude, higher positive mood, and lower systolic blood pressure during stress recovery than breathing just 1 breath/min faster (RF + 1).

Frontiers in Public Health (2017) · PMID: 28890735 · PMC5575449  ·  Frontiers in Psychology (2014) · PMID: 25101026 · PMC4104929

3. Inhale-to-Exhale Ratio & Vagal Tone (Bae et al., 2021; Van Diest et al., 2014)

During inhalation, cardiac vagal brake is suppressed (heart rate accelerates); during exhalation, acetylcholine release at the sinoatrial node restores vagal braking. Slow breathing with a 40:60 inhale-to-exhale ratio at ~6 bpm produces stronger high-frequency (HF) parasympathetic HRV modulation and subjective relaxation than 50:50 pacing.

Zaccaro et al., Frontiers in Human Neuroscience (2018) · PMID: 30337860 · PMC6137615
